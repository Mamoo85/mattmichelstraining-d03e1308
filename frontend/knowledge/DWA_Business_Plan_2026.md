## 1. PRODUCT RATINGS (1-5)

### Product 1: TechAlert / HireAlert — ★★★★★ (5/5)
**Your best product. Period.**

- **What it does:** Monitors LARA license databases daily, delivers verified licensed tradesperson/nurse contacts to hiring companies.
- **Why it's special:** Nobody else is doing automated LARA license monitoring → hiring alerts for Michigan businesses. This is a genuine first-mover advantage.
- **Comparable products:** ZipRecruiter ($299-$599/mo for employers), Indeed Sponsored Jobs ($5-15/click), LinkedIn Recruiter ($8,000+/year). All are job *posting* platforms. You're doing the inverse — proactive candidate *discovery*.
- **Moat:** LARA data is public but nobody else has built the scraping + scoring + delivery pipeline. The data is Michigan-specific, which actually helps — local wins in trades.
- **Price:** $99-149/mo is **underpriced** for what this delivers. One successful hire saves $3,000-15,000 vs a staffing agency's 20-30% placement fee.
- **Revenue potential:** 50 clients × $149/mo = $7,450/mo. This alone can hit your $10k goal.

### Product 2: Demand Radar / Industry Pulse — ★★★★☆ (4/5)
**Your second-best product. Unique concept, needs validation.**

- **What it does:** Scans hiring patterns across Metro Detroit trades, uses AI to predict equipment/supply needs before companies start shopping.
- **Why it's special:** Supply house reps currently rely on walk-ins and relationships. You're giving them predictive intelligence. This concept exists in enterprise ($50k+/year from providers like ZoomInfo, Bombora) but NOBODY has built it for the skilled trades vertical at $99/mo.
- **Comparable products:** ZoomInfo ($15,000-30,000/year), Bombora intent data ($2,000+/mo), 6sense ($75,000+/year). All enterprise, none trades-specific.
- **Risk:** Supply house reps are not tech-savvy buyers. Sales cycle could be long.
- **Revenue potential:** 20 supply house reps × $99/mo = $1,980/mo. Nice complement, not the primary revenue driver yet.

### Product 3: FieldDesk — ★★★☆☆ (3/5)
**Solid product, brutal market.**

- **What it does:** Dispatch, GPS tracking, invoicing, asset/contract management for field service companies.
- **Why it's honest truth:** ServiceTitan ($398/tech/mo), Jobber ($349/5 users), Housecall Pro ($79-249/mo). This market is **red ocean**. Your $199/mo flat pricing is attractive, but you're competing against companies with $100M+ in funding and thousands of integrations.
- **Differentiation:** Unlimited users at $199 flat. This matters for shops with 5-30 techs where ServiceTitan gets expensive fast.
- **Strategic role:** FieldDesk is better as a **Trojan Horse** — get them in on TechAlert, then upsell FieldDesk. Don't lead with it.
- **Revenue potential:** 10 shops × $199/mo = $1,990/mo. Hard-won revenue against established competitors.

### Product 4: Missed Call Text-Back — ★★★★☆ (4/5)
**Easy sell, high perceived value, low churn.**

- **What it does:** Auto-texts customers when you miss their call.
- **Why it's good:** 78% of customers hire the first responder. This stat alone closes deals. Simple concept, easy to explain, instant value.
- **Comparable products:** GoHighLevel ($97-297/mo), Podium ($249-599/mo), Housecall Pro (includes it in $249 plan). Your $49/mo is the cheapest standalone option.
- **Risk:** Easy for anyone to build. Low moat. But low churn once installed — people forget it's running.
- **Revenue potential:** 30 businesses × $49/mo = $1,470/mo. Great add-on, not a standalone business.

### Product 5: Dead Lead Reactivation — ★★★☆☆ (3/5)
**Pay-per-result model is attractive but operationally intensive.**

- **What it does:** Takes old lead lists, runs AI-powered SMS drip campaigns, charges $50 per positive reply.
- **Why it's risky:** TCPA compliance is real. One violation = $500-1,500 per text. You need bulletproof opt-out handling and prior express consent documentation.
- **Comparable:** Smith.ai ($140-700/mo), Podium ($249+/mo for automated messaging). Your pay-per-result model is unique and lower-risk for the buyer.
- **Revenue potential:** Highly variable. 20 campaigns × 10% response × $50 = depends on list quality.

- **Cons:** More competition for their attention, price-sensitive, seasonal demand

- **TAM:** ~180 nursing facilities in Metro Detroit, plus home health agencies

### VERDICT: **Lead with trades (70%), diversify with healthcare (30%).**
Trades gets you to $10k fastest. Healthcare is the higher-margin long-term play. Your Medicare scanner already gives you the list of desperate facilities — use it as a sales tool, not just an admin feature.

## 5. IS THIS LEGIT? WHY HASN'T ANYONE DONE IT?

### Why it's legit:
1. **Real data source:** LARA is a public database with real licensed professionals. This is not scraped social media — it's government-verified license data.
2. **Real pain point:** Skilled trades shortage is documented. BLS projects 500,000+ unfilled trade jobs by 2028. Michigan specifically has a boiler operator shortage.
3. **Real revenue model:** SaaS subscription with clear ROI. One hire via TechAlert saves $3,000-15,000 vs staffing agency fees. $99-149/mo is a no-brainer ROI.
4. **Real moat:** The combination of LARA scraping + AI scoring + availability prediction + 48-hour exclusive claims doesn't exist anywhere else.

### Why nobody else has done it:
1. **It's unsexy.** VCs don't fund "Michigan boiler operator license monitoring." Silicon Valley doesn't know LARA exists.
2. **It's niche.** ZoomInfo serves 300,000+ companies. You serve Metro Detroit trades. That niche is a feature, not a bug — it means you can own it.
3. **It requires domain expertise.** You have to know that a 1-star CMS staffing rating means a nursing home is desperate. You have to know that MIOSHA compliance gaps create urgent equipment service opportunities. Outsiders don't have this knowledge.
4. **It's hard to scale nationally.** Every state has different licensing databases. What works for LARA doesn't work for California's CSLB. This is actually your moat — anyone who tries to compete has to reverse-engineer each state's system.

### The honest risk:
- **Single-market risk:** You're tied to Metro Detroit/Michigan. Economic downturn in auto industry affects everything.
- **Regulatory risk:** LARA could block automated access. Unlikely (it's public data) but possible.
- **Execution risk:** You're one person. You need 50+ clients in 3 months. That's 4+ sales conversations per week.
