# TechAlert — Value Proposition & Sales Intel

## What Michigan LARA/MIOSHA Actually Publishes

**Data per license record:**
- Full legal name
- License type (e.g., 1st Class Boiler Operator, 2nd Class, Steam Engineer)
- License number
- License status (active, expired, revoked)
- Issue date
- Expiration date
- City/State (sometimes full address)

**What you DON'T get:**
- Phone number
- Email
- Current employer
- Whether they're job-seeking

## Why the Timing Signal Is the Real Value

The gold isn't the contact info — it's the **timing signal.**

1. **New license issued** = someone just passed their exam = new talent entering the market. A company that finds out day 1 has a head start over everyone else who won't know for weeks/months.
2. **License transferred/reissued** = someone may be changing employers or relocating to Michigan.
3. **License expired + not renewed** = someone left the trade = their former employer may need a replacement.

## How We Fill the Contact Gap (3-Layer Scanner)

1. **MIOSHA gives us the NAME + LICENSE TYPE + CITY** — the "who" and "when"
2. **Apollo people search** matches name + city → gives us email, phone, LinkedIn, current employer
3. **Job board scan** (Indeed, ZipRecruiter) catches people actively posting "looking for work"

**What the client actually receives:**
Not just "John Smith got a boiler license." It's: "John Smith, 1st Class Boiler Operator, Warren MI, currently at XYZ Mechanical — here's his LinkedIn." Ready to act on.

## Why Nobody Else Does This (Confirmed Market Gap)

### Big Recruiting Platforms (LinkedIn, Indeed, ZipRecruiter)
They make money on job POSTINGS. They don't care about license databases. Their business model is "employer posts job, candidates apply." Monitoring a state government database for new licenses is backwards from how they think.

### License Monitoring Companies (Verisys, Certemy, GCheck)
They monitor licenses of people you ALREADY EMPLOY to make sure they don't expire. That's a compliance product. They've never flipped it to say "alert me when NEW people get licensed." Different use case entirely.

### Staffing Agencies
They know about LARA data but they use it manually. They're not building software to automate it because they're staffing companies, not tech companies.

### The Technical Barrier
The data is free but messy. LARA doesn't have a clean API. You have to scrape their portal or do FOIA bulk requests. Most people don't bother.

## Legal Status

- **hiQ v. LinkedIn (9th Circuit, 2022):** Scraping publicly available data without a login wall is NOT unauthorized access under the CFAA
- **LARA bulk data:** Available via FOIA request — legally clean, no scraping needed
- **Public records are public by design.** No Michigan LARA ToS restriction on commercial use found
- **Cleanest path:** FOIA request for bulk data instead of portal scraping = zero legal risk
