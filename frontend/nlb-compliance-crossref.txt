EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

NLB Corporate Policy #1053c (Hazard Communication) and Appendix A (Spill Response
Procedures) were reviewed against regulatory requirements in three jurisdictions:

  USA:  OSHA Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200 (GHS-aligned)
  UK:   HSE - Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH),
        GB CLP Regulation, REACH UK
  UAE:  Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 (Labour Law, Chapter 5 - Occupational Safety &
        Health), JAFZA Health & Safety Guidelines, Ministerial Resolutions

FINDING: NLB Policy #1053c is well-constructed for U.S. OSHA compliance. However,
12 material gaps exist for UK operations and 11 material gaps exist for UAE/JAFZA
operations. The addendums below detail each gap with the required action.

SECTION 2: UNITED KINGDOM ADDENDUM
================================================================================

Applicable Regulations:
  - Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH) - SI 2002/2677
  - GB CLP Regulation (Classification, Labelling and Packaging) - retained EU
    Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008
  - UK REACH - Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals
  - Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999
  - Safety Representatives and Safety Committees Regulations 1977
  - Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR)
  - Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (HSWA)
  - Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002 (DSEAR)
  - Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992

ADDENDUM UK-1: COSHH Risk Assessment Requirement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: COSHH Reg. 6 - Assessment of the risk to health created by work
involving substances hazardous to health

NLB Gap: Policy #1053c relies on SDS from suppliers for hazard determination. UK
law requires a formal, documented COSHH risk assessment for every hazardous
substance or process - beyond what an SDS provides.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Conduct and document a COSHH risk assessment for each hazardous substance used
    at UK facilities
  - Assessments must consider: the nature of the substance, level/type/duration of
    exposure, circumstances of the work, conclusions from monitoring or health
    surveillance
  - Assessments must be reviewed regularly and immediately upon any material change
  - Records must be retained for a minimum of 40 years where personal exposure is
    involved

Responsible Party: UK Site EHS Coordinator, reporting to NLB Corporate EHS Manager

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-2: Workplace Exposure Limits (WELs)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: COSHH Reg. 7 and HSE document EH40/2005 (as amended)

NLB Gap: Policy references OSHA Permissible Exposure Limits (PELs). UK uses
Workplace Exposure Limits (WELs) published in EH40, which differ in values and
methodology.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - All exposure monitoring at UK sites must reference WELs (not OSHA PELs)
  - Where a WEL exists, exposure must be reduced so far as is reasonably practicable
    and, in any case, below the WEL
  - Short-term exposure limits (STELs) of 15 minutes apply for many substances
    (OSHA uses 8-hr TWA primarily)
  - UK WELs are legally binding; OSHA PELs are not directly applicable

Key Differences in Limits (examples):

  Substance                OSHA PEL (8-hr TWA)      UK WEL (8-hr TWA)
  ----------------------   ----------------------   ----------------------
  Toluene                  200 ppm                  50 ppm
  Methyl ethyl ketone      200 ppm                  200 ppm
  Isocyanates (as NCO)     0.02 ppm (ceiling)       0.02 mg/m3 (8-hr)

Note: Full WEL comparison must be prepared for all chemicals on NLB's chemical
inventory at UK sites.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-3: Health Surveillance
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: COSHH Reg. 11 - Health surveillance

NLB Gap: Policy #1053c does not address health surveillance. UK requires mandatory
health surveillance for employees exposed to specific hazardous substances.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Implement a health surveillance program for workers exposed to substances listed
    in COSHH Schedule 6 (e.g., isocyanates, certain solvents, lead compounds,
    chromium compounds)
  - Health surveillance must be conducted by a qualified occupational health
    professional
  - Individual health records must be maintained for at least 40 years
  - Workers have the right to access their health records

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-4: GB CLP Labeling Requirements
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: GB CLP Regulation (retained EU Reg. 1272/2008, as amended for GB)

NLB Gap: Policy references OSHA GHS labeling and HMIS labels. UK requires
compliance with GB CLP, which is GHS-aligned but has GB-specific requirements
post-Brexit.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Labels at UK sites must comply with GB CLP (not US OSHA GHS or HMIS)
  - GB CLP requires: product identifier, signal word, hazard statements, precautionary
    statements, GHS pictograms, supplier identification, and GB-specific UFI (Unique
    Formula Identifier) where applicable
  - HMIS labels are NOT recognized in the UK - do not use as a substitute for GB
    CLP labels
  - Poison Centre notification may be required for certain mixtures

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-5: UK REACH - Chemical Registration
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: UK REACH (retained EU Reg. 1907/2006, as amended for GB)

NLB Gap: Not addressed in Policy #1053c.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Any hazardous chemicals manufactured in or imported to the UK in quantities above
    1 tonne/year must be registered under UK REACH with the Health and Safety
    Executive
  - Downstream users must check that their use is covered in the supplier's
    registration
  - Safety Data Sheets provided to UK sites must comply with UK REACH Annex II format

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-6: Competent Person Requirement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, Reg. 7

NLB Gap: Policy assigns responsibility to the "Environmental, Health, & Safety
Manager." UK law specifically requires the appointment of one or more competent
persons with sufficient training, experience, and knowledge.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Formally appoint a "Competent Person" for UK operations (may be the UK Site
    Manager or a dedicated H&S officer)
  - Competent Person must hold a recognized UK qualification (e.g., NEBOSH National
    General Certificate minimum, NEBOSH Diploma preferred)
  - Document the appointment and qualifications

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-7: Safety Representatives and Consultation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: Safety Representatives and Safety Committees Regulations 1977; Health
and Safety (Consultation with Employees) Regulations 1996

NLB Gap: Not addressed in Policy #1053c.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - If a recognized trade union is present, union-appointed Safety Representatives
    have legal rights to: inspect the workplace, investigate incidents, attend safety
    committee meetings, and be consulted on health and safety matters
  - If no trade union is present, NLB must consult directly with employees or their
    elected representatives on matters affecting their health and safety
  - This includes consultation on any changes to hazardous chemicals, processes, or
    procedures

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-8: RIDDOR Incident Reporting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations
2013 (RIDDOR)

NLB Gap: Appendix A addresses internal spill reporting but not external regulatory
reporting. UK requires mandatory reporting to HSE.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Report to HSE (via online portal) any:
      o Dangerous occurrence involving hazardous substances (including uncontrolled
        releases)
      o Occupational disease caused by chemical exposure
      o Injury resulting from chemical exposure that leads to over-7-day
        incapacitation
      o Fatal or specified injuries
  - Reports must be made without delay (within 10 days for over-7-day injuries)
  - Records of all reportable incidents must be retained for at least 3 years

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-9: Bilingual/Multilingual Training Requirement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: HSWA 1974, s.2; COSHH Reg. 12(4)

NLB Gap: Policy states labels must be "in English (although other languages may
also be included)." UK law requires training and information to be provided in a
language understood by the worker.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - All HazCom training must be delivered in a language the employee understands
  - Translated SDS summaries or toolbox talks must be available for non-English-
    speaking workers
  - This is a legal duty, not optional

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-10: DSEAR - Explosive Atmospheres
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002 (DSEAR)

NLB Gap: Not addressed in Policy #1053c.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Where flammable or explosive substances are used (paints, solvents, thinners -
    referenced in Appendix A), a DSEAR risk assessment is required
  - ATEX zoning must be implemented where explosive atmospheres may form
  - This is in addition to COSHH assessments

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-11: Emergency Plan Requirements
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: COSHH Reg. 13 - Arrangements to deal with accidents, incidents, and
emergencies

NLB Gap: Appendix A covers spill response but does not meet the prescriptive
requirements of COSHH Reg. 13.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Develop a UK-specific emergency plan that includes:
      o Specific emergency procedures for foreseeable chemical incidents (not just
        spills)
      o Identification of employees designated to carry out emergency procedures
      o Availability and testing of first-aid and decontamination facilities
      o Information on specific hazards available to emergency services responding
        to incidents
      o Regular drills and testing of emergency procedures (documented)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UK-12: Spill Response - Environmental Reporting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: Environmental Permitting Regulations 2016; Environmental Protection Act
1990, s.34

NLB Gap: Appendix A addresses internal environmental contamination consideration
but does not address UK environmental reporting obligations.

Required Action for UK Operations:
  - Any spill that enters or threatens to enter controlled waters, drains, or land
    must be reported immediately to the Environment Agency (England) or Natural
    Resources Wales
  - Duty of Care for waste disposal under s.34 EPA 1990 - all spill waste must be
    classified, handled, and disposed of via licensed waste carriers with appropriate
    Waste Transfer Notes
  - Hazardous waste must be consigned through registered hazardous waste carriers

SECTION 3: UNITED ARAB EMIRATES / JAFZA ADDENDUM
================================================================================

Applicable Regulations:
  - UAE Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 (Labour Law) - Chapter 5: Safety, Occupational
    Health and Social Care
  - UAE Federal Decree Law No. 33 of 2021 (updated Labour Law provisions)
  - Ministerial Resolution No. 401 of 2015 (Midday Work Ban)
  - Ministerial Resolution No. 591 of 2016 (Worker Housing Standards)
  - JAFZA Health & Safety Guidelines (Jebel Ali Free Zone Authority)
  - Dubai Civil Defence regulations
  - UAE Federal Law No. 24 of 1999 (Protection and Development of the Environment)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-1: JAFZA HSE Registration
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: JAFZA Rules & Regulations, Health & Safety Guidelines

NLB Gap: Not addressed in Policy #1053c.

Required Action for UAE/JAFZA Operations:
  - Register with JAFZA HSE Department as a licensed entity
  - Submit NLB's HSE plan to JAFZA for approval prior to commencing operations
  - Appoint a designated HSE Officer for the JAFZA facility (must be approved by
    JAFZA)
  - JAFZA conducts periodic inspections - NLB must maintain compliance at all times
  - Annual HSE performance reports must be submitted to JAFZA

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-2: Midday Work Ban - Heat Stress
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: UAE Ministerial Resolution No. 401 of 2015

NLB Gap: Not addressed in Policy #1053c. No equivalent exists in OSHA or UK HSE.

Required Action for UAE Operations:
  - OUTDOOR WORK IS PROHIBITED BETWEEN 12:30 PM AND 3:00 PM FROM JUNE 15 TO
    SEPTEMBER 15
  - This applies to all outdoor workers, including those handling chemicals outdoors
  - Employers must provide shaded rest areas, potable water, and cooling facilities
  - Heat stress first-aid training must be provided to all outdoor workers
  - Violations are subject to fines of AED 5,000 per worker per incident and
    potential business suspension

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-3: Arabic Language Requirement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: UAE Federal Law No. 8 of 1980, Art. 4; JAFZA HSE Guidelines

NLB Gap: Policy states labels must be "in English (although other languages may
also be included)." UAE requires Arabic.

Required Action for UAE Operations:
  - All safety signage, chemical labels, and warning signs must be in Arabic AND
    English at minimum
  - SDS documents must be available in Arabic for local regulatory submissions
  - Training materials and emergency procedures must be available in Arabic and,
    where applicable, in languages understood by the workforce (e.g., Hindi, Urdu,
    Tagalog for labor workers)
  - Arabic is the official language for all government/regulatory filings

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-4: Pre-Employment and Periodic Medical Examinations
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: UAE Federal Law No. 8 of 1980, Art. 93-94; JAFZA HSE Guidelines

NLB Gap: Policy #1053c does not address medical fitness requirements. UAE
requirements are more extensive than OSHA medical surveillance.

Required Action for UAE Operations:
  - All workers must undergo pre-employment medical fitness examinations
  - Workers exposed to hazardous chemicals must have periodic medical examinations
    (at least annually)
  - Medical fitness certificates must be maintained on file and available for JAFZA
    inspection
  - Workers found medically unfit for chemical work must be reassigned or terminated
    with appropriate benefits
  - All medical records must be retained for the duration of employment plus 5 years

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-5: Chemical Import Permits and Registration
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: JAFZA Rules & Regulations; UAE Federal Law No. 24 of 1999
(Environmental Law)

NLB Gap: Not addressed in Policy #1053c.

Required Action for UAE/JAFZA Operations:
  - All hazardous chemicals imported into JAFZA require prior approval and import
    permits from JAFZA authorities
  - A Hazardous Materials Inventory must be submitted to JAFZA upon request
  - Certain chemicals may be restricted or banned under UAE environmental law -
    cross-check NLB's chemical inventory against UAE restricted substances list
  - Material transport within JAFZA must comply with JAFZA's dangerous goods
    transportation guidelines

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-6: Dubai Civil Defence Approval
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: Dubai Civil Defence Code; JAFZA Fire Safety Requirements

NLB Gap: Appendix A addresses internal emergency response. UAE requires external
government approval of fire safety and emergency plans.

Required Action for UAE Operations:
  - Fire safety plans must be submitted to and approved by Dubai Civil Defence
  - Emergency response procedures involving hazardous chemicals must be reviewed by
    Dubai Civil Defence
  - Fire suppression and detection systems must comply with Dubai Civil Defence
    specifications (not NFPA alone)
  - Annual fire safety inspections by Dubai Civil Defence are mandatory
  - Fire drills must be conducted at intervals specified by Civil Defence (typically
    quarterly)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-7: Penalties and Criminal Liability
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: UAE Federal Law No. 8 of 1980, Arts. 98-102; UAE Penal Code

NLB Gap: Policy #1053c does not address legal penalties. UAE penalties are
significantly more severe than OSHA fines.

Required Action for UAE Operations:
  - Employees and management should be aware that UAE law imposes:
      o Criminal penalties including imprisonment for safety violations resulting
        in injury or death
      o Fines ranging from AED 10,000 to AED 1,000,000 for safety violations
      o Potential deportation of responsible foreign nationals
      o Facility closure orders for repeat or serious violations
  - NLB should engage UAE legal counsel to review liability exposure

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-8: Worker Accommodation Standards
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: Ministerial Resolution No. 591 of 2016

NLB Gap: Not addressed in Policy #1053c. Only applicable if NLB provides housing.

Required Action for UAE Operations (if applicable):
  - If NLB provides worker accommodation in the UAE, facilities must meet minimum
    standards for: space per worker, ventilation, sanitation, kitchen/dining,
    recreation areas, and fire safety
  - Hazardous chemicals and PPE must not be stored in accommodation areas
  - Regular inspections of accommodation facilities are required by JAFZA

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-9: Contractor HSE Management
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: JAFZA HSE Guidelines - Contractor Management

NLB Gap: Policy addresses multi-employer worksites and contractor SDS sharing.
JAFZA requirements are more prescriptive.

Required Action for UAE/JAFZA Operations:
  - All contractors operating at NLB's JAFZA facility must submit their own HSE
    plan to JAFZA for approval
  - Permit-to-Work (PTW) system is required for all contractor activities involving
    hazardous chemicals
  - NLB is responsible for auditing contractor HSE compliance within its JAFZA
    facility
  - Contractor workers must receive site-specific induction covering NLB's chemical
    hazards

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-10: SDS Format and Submission
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: JAFZA HSE Guidelines; UAE Federal standards (GHS-aligned)

NLB Gap: Policy relies on supplier SDS in OSHA GHS format. UAE has additional
requirements.

Required Action for UAE Operations:
  - SDS documents must comply with GHS format (16-section format - aligned with
    OSHA)
  - Arabic translation of SDS must be available for regulatory submissions
  - JAFZA may request copies of SDS for any chemicals stored or used at the facility
  - SDS must be readily accessible to workers at all times (not just "available
    online")

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDENDUM UAE-11: Environmental Spill Reporting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regulation: UAE Federal Law No. 24 of 1999; JAFZA Environmental Guidelines

NLB Gap: Appendix A addresses internal spill response but does not address UAE
environmental reporting obligations.

Required Action for UAE Operations:
  - Any spill that affects or threatens the environment must be reported immediately
    to JAFZA Environmental Department
  - Spills affecting marine environments (Jebel Ali port area) must also be reported
    to the Federal Environmental Agency
  - Environmental remediation costs are the responsibility of the polluter
  - Failure to report environmental contamination is a criminal offense under UAE law
