What LOs Are Missing

Metro Detroit closes approximately 34,000+ home sales per year across Wayne, Oakland, Macomb, Washtenaw, Monroe, and Livingston counties. At an average loan amount of $220,000 and a 1.5% commission, that's roughly $112M in annual LO commissions distributed across the market every year.

The problem is that 90% of LOs in SE Michigan are fighting over the same referral channel: listing agent relationships. When those agents have a preferred LO, you get nothing. When they retire, you start over. When the market slows, the referrals dry up.

Section 02

The 4 Lead Sources That Generate
Purchase Intent Borrowers Every Week

🏷️

FSBO (For Sale By Owner) Sellers

Metro Detroit has an active FSBO market — roughly 18% of home sales involve FSBO at some stage. FSBO sellers are motivated (they're actively trying to close a transaction) and almost always need to simultaneously purchase their next home, which means they need an LO. Most LOs ignore them because "they don't work with realtors." That's exactly why the opportunity exists. FSBO sellers list on Zillow, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, and local FSBO sites — all publicly scrapable.

📋 Signal type: Active seller with concurrent purchase intent
⚖️

Probate & Estate Sales

Wayne County Probate Court processes 3,500–4,000 estate cases per year, a significant percentage of which involve real property. When a homeowner inherits a property, they face three choices: keep it, rent it, or sell it. The ones who sell are highly motivated — they didn't plan for this transaction, they want to close fast, and they need financing for their next property if they're buying with proceeds. Probate filings are public record in Michigan. Most LOs never look at them.

📋 Signal type: Motivated seller with asset liquidity event
📜

Divorce-Driven Property Sales

Wayne County alone processes 6,200+ divorce filings annually. Oakland County adds another 3,800+. Michigan divorce proceedings that involve real property almost always require a property disposition decision — sell and split proceeds, or one party buys out the other. Both scenarios involve mortgage financing. The divorce attorney is the decision-driver here, not the realtor. LOs who build relationships with family law attorneys in Bloomfield Hills, Royal Oak, and Grosse Pointe get a consistent stream of pre-qualified borrowers with urgent timelines.

⚡ Signal type: Court-mandated timeline, highly motivated
📉

Pre-Foreclosure & Distress Signals

CFPB Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) data shows refinance origination spikes by ZIP code — a leading indicator of homeowner financial stress before it becomes public. Wayne County Probate Court Chapter 13 filings (bankruptcy with a repayment plan) are public record and often precede either a property sale or a refinance. These are borrowers who need creative solutions — and the LO who reaches them first with a real option earns their trust and their business.

📋 Signal type: Financial event creating urgent financing need

Signal Timing Windows:
When to Act on Each Source

Every lead source has a different window of opportunity. Acting too early means the homeowner isn't ready. Acting too late means a competitor already has the relationship. Here are the optimal timing windows for SE Michigan LOs:

FSBO

Contact within 48 hours of listing appearing

FSBO listings typically generate the most qualified contacts in the first week. By Day 14, FSBO sellers have often either accepted an offer or decided to list with an agent. Your window is tight. Message: "I work with buyers who are pre-approved and ready to move quickly — I can have a pre-approval letter to your buyer within 24 hours. Would it be helpful to have my number for when they ask?"

Probate

Wait 30–45 days after filing before outreach

Probate filings in Wayne County take 4–6 weeks minimum before the estate administrator has clarity on assets and obligations. Reaching out during the first 30 days feels predatory and gets ignored. Reaching out at 30–45 days — when they're starting to make decisions — positions you as a resource. Message: "I specialize in helping families navigate property decisions during estate settlement — no pressure, just want to make sure financing is one less thing to worry about."

Divorce

Cultivate attorney relationships before the need arises

You can't time individual divorce filings. What you can do is become the go-to LO for 5–10 family law attorneys in Bloomfield Hills, Birmingham, and Grosse Pointe. Lunch, a referral fee structure (where compliant), or simply being the person they call because you're responsive. One attorney with 100 annual divorce cases that involve property = 30–50 annual referrals. That's a pipeline worth more than most marketing budgets.

Pre-Foreclosure

90–120 days before auction date

Michigan foreclosures go through a 6-month statutory redemption period after the sheriff's sale. The optimal window to reach distressed homeowners is 90–120 days before the auction — when they still have options (refinance, sale, or workout) but the timeline is urgent enough that they'll take action. After 60 days, options narrow significantly. PACER.gov and Wayne County court records both carry this data.

Section 04

The Realtor Relationship Problem
(And How to Solve It)

Realtor referrals are the highest-quality leads in the mortgage business. The problem is that every LO in SE Michigan is competing for the same relationships, and most realtors already have 1–3 preferred LOs. Getting onto that list is hard. Staying on it requires constant cultivation.

Here's the hierarchy of what Metro Detroit realtors actually value in an LO relationship, in order:

  1. Speed — pre-approval letter in under 2 hours In a competitive Metro Detroit market (especially Oakland County, where homes sell in 4–8 days), a slow pre-approval loses deals. Realtors will switch their preferred LO over this single issue. If you can't issue a pre-approval letter same-day, you're uncompetitive for top-producing agents.
  2. Availability — respond within 30 minutes, 7 days a week Top realtors in SE Michigan work weekends. Offers get written Sunday at 7pm. If you're not reachable, the client gets pre-approved elsewhere, and the realtor remembers. This is the number-one reason LOs lose realtor relationships that they spent months building.
  3. Communication — proactive updates, not reactive responses Realtors want to hear from you before they have to ask. "Hey, your client's loan went to underwriting — we're on track for Friday closing" is worth more than a business lunch. Automate this with a simple text/email trigger at each milestone.
  4. Clean closings — no last-minute surprises One deal that falls apart at the closing table because of a preventable LO issue can end a referral relationship. Top Oakland County agents give their preferred LOs exactly one chance to blow a closing before moving on.
  5. Value-add content — market updates, rate context, buyer education Realtors who send their clients rate commentary, loan program updates, and buyer education content from their LO partner look smarter to their clients. Be the LO who makes their agents look good — not the one who just processes applications.
The 10-agent strategy: Instead of trying to get referrals from 50 mediocre agents, identify the top 10 producing realtors in your target ZIP codes in Wayne and Oakland County. These are agents closing 25+ transactions per year. Court 10 of them with genuine value (market data, fast pre-approvals, co-hosted first-time buyer webinars). Winning 4 of those 10 as regular referral partners is worth more than 100 passive agent relationships.

SE Michigan Housing Market Timing:
When to Push and When to Conserve

Metro Detroit's housing market has a distinct seasonal pattern that most LOs in the market already know but few actually align their marketing spend with:

Jan–Feb

Rate sensitivity — refi opportunity window

Purchase volume is lowest. Homeowners are receptive to rate conversations. This is the time to call past clients for refinance check-ins and to run your FSBO and probate outreach sequences — those sellers are starting to plan spring listings. Don't pay for purchase-focused ads. Do stay active with relationship outreach.

Mar–Apr

🔥 Ramp season — get your pipeline loaded

SE Michigan listings start hitting MLS in earnest starting mid-March. Buyers who've been pre-approved through the winter are ready to move. This is the time to be most visible — in realtor offices, co-hosting buyer seminars, showing up at open houses, running targeted Facebook ads for first-time buyers in 48xxx ZIP codes. The buyers who pre-approve in March close in May and June.

May–Jul

🔥🔥 Peak purchase season

Highest transaction volume of the year. Every hour you're not working a deal is potential revenue gone. Focus entirely on execution and service. The LOs who close the most deals in this window are the ones who built their pipeline in Q1. Service quality here determines your Q4 referral volume — satisfied clients from spring closings send referrals in the fall.

Aug–Sep

Second wave — families buying before school year

The Oakland County and Macomb County suburban markets see a second peak in August as families try to close before the school year. Less competitive than May–June but still strong. FSBO sellers who didn't move in spring are more motivated by September — price reductions and seller urgency create refi/purchase opportunities.

Oct–Dec

Rebuild relationships, plant seeds for spring

Activity slows. Use this quarter to deepen your 10 realtor relationships, run your past-client check-in campaign, and refresh your FSBO/probate pipeline. The LOs who are most visible to realtors in Q4 are the ones who get the early spring listings in Q1.
