Section 02

Reading Storm Signals:
The 9-Minute Window

In SE Michigan, when a significant hail or wind storm hits, there's a 9-minute window that separates contractors who book 15–20 jobs from contractors who get none. Here's what happens in real-time:

Hour 0

Storm makes contact

NOAA's Storm Prediction Center logs the event. Large hail (1"+ diameter) and sustained wind events above 58 mph are the primary triggers for insurance-eligible roof claims in SE Michigan.

Hour 0–9

The first-mover window

The contractors who call or text homeowners in the affected ZIP codes within the first 9 hours book the majority of free inspections. This is not an exaggeration — roofing is more time-competitive than any other trade in SE Michigan after a storm event.

Hours 9–48

Storm chasers from Ohio and Indiana arrive

Out-of-state crews — primarily from Cincinnati, Columbus, and Indianapolis — start canvassing neighborhoods in vans within 24–48 hours of major events. They offer low prices, no local warranty, and often disappear after collecting the insurance payout. This is your enemy. Beat them with local presence and faster response.

Day 3–14

Homeowners start calling independently

After the first wave of shock, homeowners who didn't respond to initial canvassing start searching Google. This is the second window — where your GBP and website SEO determine whether they call you or a competitor.

Day 7–30

Permit pulls begin

When a roofer is hired and pulls a permit with the City of Detroit (BSEED) or county building department, that permit record becomes publicly visible — and it signals which addresses have recently replaced their roof AND which neighboring addresses might still need to. Permit data is free public information.

The intelligence advantage: Most contractors wait for storms to hit and then scramble. The move is to know a storm is coming (NOAA SPC issues Day-1 convective outlooks 24 hours in advance), have your outreach templates ready, and send targeted messages to homeowners in the affected ZIPs within hours of impact — before the storm chasers from Cincinnati pull into the neighborhood.

Section 03

The 5 Signal Types That Generate
Roofing Leads Every Single Week

Storm signals are the most visible but also the most competitive. Here are the five signal types that generate qualified leads in SE Michigan — including four that most roofing contractors completely ignore:

⛈️

Hail & Wind Storm Events (NOAA SPC)

SE Michigan averages 2.1 significant hail events per year. Hail diameter ≥1" triggers insurance claims on most standard homeowner policies. The SPC Day-1 Convective Outlook publishes 24 hours in advance — meaning you can prepare messaging before impact, not after. Filter for Wayne, Oakland, Macomb counties. Available free at spc.noaa.gov.

⚡ Urgency Score: 9/10 — Act same day
🏠

New Homeowners (Property Deed Transfers)

Wayne County property deed transfer records are public. When a home changes hands — especially a pre-1990 home — the new owner is highly motivated to assess the roof condition within 6–18 months. They're not emotionally attached to the previous owner's deferred maintenance decisions. Average job value from new-homeowner leads: $8,000–$14,000. Competition level: very low.

📋 Urgency Score: 6/10 — Opportunity window: 3–6 months
📋

Building Permit Activity (BSEED / County Records)

When a homeowner pulls a building permit for any significant home improvement — addition, structural work, major renovation — it often surfaces an adjacent roofing need. The City of Detroit's BSEED permit database is public and searchable. Roofing permits pulled in the last 30 days also show you which streets have "social proof" momentum — neighbors who see one new roof tend to call roofers within 90 days.

📋 Urgency Score: 5/10 — Neighborhood momentum signal
🏷️

Pre-Sale Inspections (BSEED Residential CoFC)

Detroit's BSEED Certificate of Compliance (CoFC) program requires a pre-sale inspection for residential properties before a sale can close. An expiring or recently-issued CoFC is one of the most under-used roofing lead signals in the market. A homeowner who needs to pass inspection to sell their house is highly motivated to fix roof deficiencies fast — they're on a deadline. Over 11,000 active CofC records exist in the BSEED database.

📋 Urgency Score: 8/10 — Motivated seller, hard deadline
📡

FEMA Disaster Declarations & Flood Zone Activity

SE Michigan has received 14 FEMA disaster declarations since 2000 — primarily for flood and severe storm events. Post-disaster declarations open the FEMA Hazard Mitigation Grant Program (HMGP), which funds residential roof replacements and structural hardening in affected counties. This creates a window of highly-motivated homeowners with access to grant funding — not insurance, actual federal grants — for roof work. Most roofers in SE Michigan don't know this exists.

📋 Urgency Score: 7/10 — FEMA window typically 90–180 days

Section 05

The SE Michigan Roofing
Timing Calendar

If you market evenly year-round, you're wasting money 8 months a year. Here's the exact timing pattern for SE Michigan roofing revenue — and how to spend your marketing dollars against it:

Jan–Mar

Dead season — prepare, don't market

Almost no residential roofing happens in SE Michigan Jan–Mar. Ice dams create emergency calls (usually tarp work, not replacement). Use this time to build your Google reviews, update your GBP, and finalize your storm-response playbook so it's ready when April arrives. Don't pay for Google Ads this quarter.

April

🔥 Pre-season — your most valuable 30 days

Post-winter damage assessments begin. Homeowners survey their roofs after snowmelt. Hail season starts. This is when you want your GBP fully updated, your review count highest, and your ad campaigns live. If you miss April, you miss 20% of the year's opportunity. Budget accordingly.

May–Jun

🔥🔥 Peak season — maximum volume

Highest booking volume of the year. Peak hail window for SE Michigan. Insurance claim processing in full swing from April storms. The top 3 roofers in Metro Detroit book 40–60% of their annual jobs in these two months. Spend heavily here. Run Google Local Services Ads. Keep your crew schedule full 6 weeks out.

Jul–Aug

Mid-season — insurance claims processing + new homeowners

Steady demand from Q2 storm claims still being processed. Summer is when new homeowners (bought in spring) start getting roof assessments. Good target: homes in 48xx4, 48xx5, 48xx8 ZIP ranges that transferred in Q1–Q2. These are your uncontested leads — no storm chasers competing, just you calling a new homeowner with a 30-year-old roof.

Sep–Oct

Close-out season — urgency pricing works here

Homeowners who put off summer repairs start feeling urgency as winter approaches. "Get it done before snow" is a legitimate and honest sales message. Permit pull activity stays solid through October. Run a September push on your email list and past customers — "Last open slots before winter" generates real response in SE Michigan.

Nov–Dec

Wind down — emergency only

Installation weather becomes marginal after mid-November. November storms can create emergency tarp calls which are high-margin short jobs. December is planning month — update your website, refresh photos, plan your spring ad spend. Don't run campaigns.
