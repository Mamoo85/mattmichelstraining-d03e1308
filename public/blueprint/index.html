Section 01

Why Most Contractor Websites
Lose You Jobs Every Day

You're busy. You're good at what you do. And somewhere along the way someone told you that you needed a website — so you got one. Maybe a cousin built it, maybe you paid a marketing company $3,000, maybe Wix auto-generated one from a template.

Here's the problem: most contractor websites are built to look good in a portfolio, not to get your phone ringing. There's a difference, and it costs you jobs daily.

After auditing hundreds of contractor websites across Metro Detroit, the same three root causes show up every time:

📵
No Clear Call to Action
A visitor lands on your site. There's no phone number above the fold, no "Book Now" button, no reason to act. They leave in 8 seconds. You never knew they were there.
👻
Google Can't Find You
Your site has no local SEO setup. No city pages, no Google Business Profile linked, no schema markup. A homeowner in your ZIP code Googles "HVAC repair near me" and you don't exist.
📞
Missed Calls = Lost Jobs
The phone rings at 7pm. You're on a job. No voicemail response system. No auto-text back. That homeowner called the next guy on the list. You lost a $4,000 job.
The real cost: A typical HVAC, roofing, or plumbing job is $2,000–$8,000. If your website is losing you just 2 jobs per month, that's $4,000–$16,000 walking out the door. Monthly.
Section 02

The 5 Non-Negotiables of a
High-Converting Contractor Site

These aren't nice-to-haves. Every contractor website that consistently generates leads has all five. If yours is missing even one, you're bleeding calls.

1

Phone Number in the Top-Right Corner, Always

Not in the footer. Not buried on a contact page. Top-right, every page, click-to-call on mobile. This single change lifts inbound calls 20–40% in the first week. Period.

2

A Hero Section That Says What You Do and Where

Within 3 seconds of landing on your site, a stranger should know: what trade you're in, what cities you serve, and how to contact you right now. "Welcome to Joe's HVAC" tells them nothing. "Same-Day HVAC Repair — Detroit, Warren, Dearborn" tells them everything.

3

Google Reviews Front and Center

Homeowners don't trust contractors they don't know. Your Google rating is your credibility. Embed your live Google review widget on the homepage. If you have fewer than 20 reviews, that's your first priority — see Section 6 for the automation setup.

4

A Fast, Simple Contact Form That Actually Works

Not a 12-field form asking for their mother's maiden name. Name, phone, and "What do you need?" — that's it. Every additional field you add drops conversions by 11%. Test your form quarterly. We've seen contractor sites where the form was broken for 8 months.

5

Mobile-First Design (70%+ of Your Traffic Is Phone)

Pull up your website on your phone right now. Can you tap the phone number? Does it load in under 3 seconds? Is the text readable without zooming? If any answer is no, you're losing a majority of your visitors before they ever read a word.

Quick test: Google "PageSpeed Insights" and run your website URL. A score below 70 on mobile means you're likely ranking below competitors with faster sites. Google uses page speed as a ranking factor.
Section 03

Local SEO in 30 Minutes:
The Google Business Profile Checklist

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single highest-ROI thing you can control for free. When someone searches "roofer near me" or "HVAC repair Detroit," the 3-pack of businesses that shows up is driven entirely by GBP signals.

Here's the 30-minute setup that gets results. Do this once and maintain it monthly:

0–5 min

Claim and verify your listing

Go to business.google.com. Search for your business. If it exists, claim it. If not, create it. Choose your primary category carefully — "HVAC Contractor" outperforms "Air Conditioning Contractor" in most markets.

5–10 min

Fill every field completely

Business name (exactly as it appears on your truck), phone, website, hours, service area (list every city you serve — up to 20). Incomplete profiles rank 40% lower than fully complete ones.

10–15 min

Add real photos of your work

Upload at least 10 job photos — before/after pairs whenever possible. Businesses with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks than those without. Your iPhone is fine. Stop waiting for professional photos.

15–20 min

Write your business description using keywords

750 characters. Include your trade, your service area cities, and what makes you different. Example: "Family-owned HVAC contractor serving Detroit, Warren, Sterling Heights, and Macomb County since 2009. Same-day emergency service, financing available, licensed and insured."

20–25 min

Add your services with descriptions

Click "Services" in your GBP dashboard. Add every service you offer with a 100-word description each. This is free keyword real estate Google uses to match you to searches. "Furnace repair," "AC installation," "duct cleaning" — each one is a separate opportunity to rank.

25–30 min

Post a Google Update (do this weekly after)

Go to "Posts" and add a Google Update — a recent job photo with 2–3 sentences. "Just finished a full furnace replacement in Sterling Heights — homeowner was quoted $4,200 by a competitor, we came in at $2,800 and finished same day." This keeps your profile active and Google rewards it.

Ongoing (5 min/week): Reply to every Google review within 24 hours — positive or negative. Google tracks response rate. Businesses that respond to 100% of reviews rank higher than those who don't respond at all.

The Missed-Call Problem
(And the Fix)

Here's what happens when your phone rings and you don't answer: the homeowner waits 90 seconds, hangs up, and calls the next contractor in their Google search results. By the time you call back — even 20 minutes later — they've already booked someone else.

This isn't a you problem. Every contractor deals with this. You can't answer the phone when you're under a house or on a roof. The question is whether your system handles it for you.

The fix has two parts:

  • Auto-text reply within 60 seconds: When a call goes unanswered, an automated text fires immediately: "Hey, this is [Your Name] with [Business]. I saw you just called — I'm on a job right now but I'll call you back within the hour. What's the best number to reach you?" This alone recovers 30–40% of missed calls.
  • Voicemail-to-text transcription: Every voicemail transcribed and texted to your phone in real time. You read it in 10 seconds, decide priority, and either call back immediately or text them. No more "checking voicemail at the end of the day."
  • After-hours routing: Calls after 8pm route to a recorded message that captures name, number, and job type. Morning response text goes out automatically at 7am.
Real example: A Detroit plumber we work with was missing 4–6 calls per week. After setting up auto-text reply, he recovered 3 of those per week on average — that's roughly $9,000–$15,000/month in jobs he was previously losing silently.
Warning: If you're spending money on Google Ads or HomeAdvisor and not fixing your missed-call problem first, you're paying to generate leads you're then losing. Fix the leak before you turn on the faucet.
Section 05

Review Generation on Autopilot

Google reviews are the single biggest trust signal for a local contractor. More reviews = higher ranking = more calls. It's that simple. And yet most contractors have 8–12 reviews, while their top competitor has 200+.

The difference isn't that your competitor has more satisfied customers. It's that they have a system that automatically asks.

The 3-text review system (set this up today):

  1. Job completion text (Day 0) Send within 2 hours of finishing: "Hey [Name], it's [Your Name] — just wanted to make sure everything is working perfectly with the [job]. Any questions at all, text me directly. And if you have a second, a Google review means the world to a small business like mine: [your Google review link]"
  2. Follow-up text (Day 3) If no review yet: "Hi [Name] — [Your Name] here, just checking in. Everything still good with the [job]? And if you haven't had a chance, I'd really appreciate a quick Google review: [link]. Takes 60 seconds."
  3. Final text (Day 7) "Last time I'll ask — if you'd be willing to leave us a Google review, it directly helps us compete against the big national companies. Here's the link: [link]. Thank you either way."
Getting your Google review link: Search your business name on Google → click your listing → click "Write a review" → copy the URL from the popup. Shorten it with bit.ly for texting.

This system, on its own, generates 8–15 new reviews per month for an average contractor doing 20 jobs/month. At that rate, you'll have 100+ reviews within a year — which puts you in the Google 3-pack for most local search terms.

Responding to bad reviews: Never ignore them. Never argue. Reply within 24 hours: "Hi [Name], I'm sorry your experience didn't meet our standards. I'd like to make this right — please call me directly at (313) 992-1219." One graceful response to a bad review converts more skeptical readers than five 5-star reviews.

Section 06

The 90-Day Launch Checklist

This is the exact sequence we walk new clients through. Do these in order — each phase builds on the last.

Days 1–30
Foundation
  • Claim and fully complete your Google Business Profile (Section 3 checklist)
  • Fix or build your website — phone number top-right, mobile-optimized, contact form working
  • Set up Google Analytics and Google Search Console (free — takes 20 minutes)
  • Start the 3-text review system for every completed job
  • Set up voicemail-to-text (Google Voice is free; dedicated systems start at $29/mo)
  • Add your business to Bing Places, Yelp, and Angi (free listings)
Days 31–60
Growth
  • Post one Google Update per week (job photo + 2-sentence description)
  • Add 3–5 city-specific pages to your website (e.g. "HVAC Repair Warren MI")
  • Respond to all reviews within 24 hours — positive and negative
  • Set up auto-text reply for missed calls
  • Build 5–10 citations: same business name/address/phone on directories (BBB, HomeAdvisor, etc.)
  • Ask your 10 best past clients for a review with a personal text
Days 61–90
Scale
  • Check Google Search Console — what keywords are getting impressions? Double down on those.
  • Add before/after photos from your best jobs to your website and GBP
  • Run a $5/day Google Local Services Ad (pay-per-call, Google-verified leads)
  • Set up a simple email capture on your website ("Get a Free Estimate") — start building your list
  • Review your Google Analytics: which pages drive the most conversions? Push more traffic there.
  • Ask 5 satisfied clients for a video testimonial (phone video, 30 seconds — it's gold)
Track one number: Inbound calls per month. Every initiative above should move that number. If it doesn't, adjust. If it does, double it.
Section 07

What to Look for When
Hiring a Web Agency

If you're ready to hand this off to someone who does it professionally, here's how to avoid getting burned. The web design industry is full of people who charge $5,000 for a template they bought for $59.

Ask these 5 questions before you write a check:

  1. Can you show me 3 contractor sites you've built that are currently ranking on Google? Anyone can build a pretty site. You need one that ranks. If they can't show you live examples with Google rankings, walk away.
  2. What do I own after this engagement? You should own your domain name, your Google account, your website files, and all your logins — period. If an agency owns any of these "on your behalf," that's a hostage situation, not a service.
  3. What's included in the monthly fee, exactly? Get it in writing. "Maintenance" means nothing. "2 hours of updates per month, hosting, SSL, plugin updates, and weekly backup" means something.
  4. Do you have experience with my specific trade? HVAC websites need different content and keywords than roofing websites. A generalist agency will give you a generic site. A specialist will give you a site that speaks to your customer's exact problem at 2am when their furnace goes out.
  5. How do you measure success? If the answer is "traffic" or "rankings," that's not enough. The only metric that matters for a contractor is inbound calls and booked jobs. Make sure your agency is tracking that, not just vanity metrics.

Red flags to avoid:

Long-term contracts (12+ months with no performance clause) · Agencies that won't give you your own Google access · "We'll build it on our platform" (you can never leave) · No case studies from your industry · Pricing that requires a salesperson to explain it
